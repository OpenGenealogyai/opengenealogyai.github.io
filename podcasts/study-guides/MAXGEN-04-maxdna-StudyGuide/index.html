MaxDNA Study Guide: Genetic Evidence and Privacy in Genealogy

This study guide provides a comprehensive overview of the MaxDNA schema and its application in genetic genealogy. It synthesizes the technical architecture, privacy protections, and methodological standards required to integrate DNA evidence with documentary genealogical research.

Overview of Key Concepts

The Role of DNA in Modern Genealogy

DNA evidence serves as a powerful supplement to traditional documentary research. While a document might suggest a relationship, DNA can confirm findings, resolve conflicting hypotheses, and identify previously unknown relatives. In the context of MaxDNA, genetic evidence is used to raise the confidence levels of genealogical assertions, particularly when combined with records in the MaxPerson schema.

The Metadata-Only Mandate

A fundamental principle of the MaxDNA architecture is that it never stores raw genotype data. Files such as FASTQ, VCF, or raw downloads from consumer platforms (e.g., 23andMe, AncestryDNA) are strictly prohibited. The field raw_genotype_stored is a constant value of false. Instead, MaxDNA stores metadata, including match relationships, shared centimorgans (cM), haplogroups, and ancestry estimates.

Privacy and Security

Because genetic data contains information about biological relatives who may not have consented to testing, MaxDNA employs the most restrictive privacy settings available: * Tier2-Private: All MaxDNA records are restricted from public datasets, APIs, and embeddings. This applies to both living individuals and historical figures. * Hashed Identifiers: DNA kit numbers are never stored in plain text. They are transformed using HMAC-SHA-256 with a system salt to prevent brute-force reversal. * Consent and Deletion: The system supports various consent statuses. A status of "withdrawn" initiates a 30-day purge cycle, after which the record is permanently deleted.

Methodological Precision

To ensure data from different testing platforms can be accurately compared, MaxDNA tracks specific methodological details: * Recombination Maps: Different maps (e.g., HapMap, deCODE) yield different centimorgan values for the same physical DNA segment. MaxDNA records the cm_map_version to allow for corrections. * Phasing: Determining whether a match is paternal or maternal is critical for triangulation. MaxDNA distinguishes between "unphased" (not attempted) and "unknown" (attempted but failed) statuses. * Endogamy: In populations that historically married within small communities, standard relationship algorithms are often inaccurate. The endogamy_flag alerts downstream tools to apply adjusted estimates.

Short-Answer Practice Questions

1. What is the primary reason MaxDNA utilizes HMAC-SHA-256 instead of standard SHA-256 for kit identifiers? A plain hash can be reversed through brute force if the kit ID format is known. An HMAC with a secret system salt renders the kit ID irreversible.

2. Explain the difference between the phasing statuses "unphased" and "unknown." "Unphased" indicates that the process of determining which parent contributed the shared segment has not yet been attempted. "Unknown" indicates that phasing was attempted but the results could not be determined.

3. What happens to a record when the consent_status is set to "withdrawn"? The system triggers a 30-day purge cycle. The request time is logged in withdrawal_requested_at, and after 30 days, the record is entirely deleted rather than archived or soft-deleted.

4. Why is the cm_map_version essential for triangulating segments from different sources? Recombination maps (like deCODE or HapMap) estimate the frequency of recombination at genomic positions. These maps can differ by up to 15%. Without knowing which map was used, relationship estimates may be inaccurate when combining data from different platforms.

5. How does DNA evidence influence the confidence of a documentary claim within the MAXGEN system? DNA evidence flows into the dna_evidence[] array of a MaxPerson record. It uses a noisy-OR confidence calculation to combine the confidence of a documentary claim with the confidence of a DNA match, resulting in a higher overall confidence for the relationship assertion.

6. What are the two direct paternal and maternal lineages tracked by MaxDNA? The Y-DNA haplogroup traces the direct paternal line, and the mtDNA (mitochondrial) haplogroup traces the direct maternal line.
