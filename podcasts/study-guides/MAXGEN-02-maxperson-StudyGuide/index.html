MaxPerson Study Guide: Principles of Probabilistic Genealogy

This study guide provides a comprehensive overview of the MaxPerson schema, a data model designed to address the fundamental challenges of identity and uncertainty in genealogical research. Unlike traditional software that forces definitive "facts," MaxPerson utilizes a probabilistic framework of assertions to mirror the scientific method and the reality of historical research.

Core Concepts

1. Assertions vs. Facts

In the MaxPerson model, there are no "facts." Every piece of information is treated as an assertion—a claim made by a specific source at a specific time with a specific confidence level. * Coexistence: Multiple, even contradictory, assertions can coexist within a single record. * Scientific Modeling: This approach treats genealogical findings like scientific findings, which include confidence intervals and methodologies that can be revised or overturned by new evidence.

2. Vital Record Assertions

  • Name Assertions: Capture names as they appear in specific documents. The highest-confidence assertion drives the display name, while others remain for researcher review.
  • Birth and Death Assertions: Utilize a year range model (e.g., year_min and year_max). This allows for the representation of imprecise data, such as "about 1847" from a census, versus exact dates from a baptismal certificate.

3. Relational and External Assertions

  • Parent/Child Assertions: These are bidirectional and probabilistic. A person can have multiple candidate parents with varying confidence levels (e.g., a biological father at 0.75 confidence and a stepfather at 0.35).
  • Spouse Assertions: These require a bidirectional sync between two records, capturing relationship types, dates, places, and reasons for the relationship ending.
  • External ID Assertions: Instead of a "hard merge" with external databases (like FamilySearch or WikiTree), MaxPerson uses scored, sourced links. This prevents the silent propagation of errors from external duplicates.

4. Confidence and Merging

  • Composite Confidence: A calculated number (0.0 to 1.0) representing the overall certainty of an identity.
  • Noisy-OR Formula: A mathematical method used to combine independent assertions. It accounts for the "independence assumption," ensuring that two records derived from the same source are not over-counted.
  • Reversible Merges: When two MaxPerson records are merged, the absorbed entity persists with a merge_status: merged_away. This allows merges to be undone if new evidence proves the individuals were distinct.

5. Privacy and Events

  • The Tier2 Gate: Controlled by the is_living flag. If true, the record is shielded from public endpoints.
  • Event Assertions: Captures the "life beyond vital records," including residence, military service, and occupation. These are designed for interoperability with standard GEDCOM tags.

Short-Answer Practice Questions

  1. How does MaxPerson’s approach to "facts" differ from traditional genealogy software? Traditional software forces a decision on a single "correct" fact, whereas MaxPerson preserves uncertainty by treating all information as assertions that can coexist even if they conflict.

  2. What is the purpose of the year_min and year_max fields in a birth assertion? They allow the system to represent the uncertainty of dates found in records like censuses (e.g., "about 1847") while still accommodating exact dates from certificates.

  3. Why is the "independence assumption" critical when calculating composite confidence? It prevents the system from artificially inflating confidence when the same document is indexed across multiple databases. MaxPerson tracks source_record_ids to detect and discount correlated evidence.

  4. Explain the bidirectional requirement for spouse assertions. If Person A is asserted as married to Person B, the assertion must appear in both records to maintain data integrity across the schema.

  5. What happens to a MaxPerson record after it has been "merged" into another? It is not destroyed; it persists with a status of merged_away in the merge_history, allowing the merge to be reversed if new evidence arises.

  6. How does the is_living flag function as a "privacy gate"? When set to true, all public endpoints return a 404 error for that record, ensuring that even if application-level filters fail, the schema protects the data of living individuals.
