MaxTask: A Standard for Distributed Genealogical Research Study Guide

This study guide provides a comprehensive overview of the MaxTask standard, a framework designed to manage and distribute genealogical research tasks across a global network of human and artificial intelligence contributors.

Key Concepts and System Overview

The Necessity of Distributed Research

The volume of historical records globally is staggering, with billions of pages held by institutions like the National Archives of the United Kingdom and the United States, the Vatican Archives, and the Mormon Church. Because no single organization or AI model can process this scale of data, genealogical research is considered "irreducibly distributed." MaxTask provides the standard for describing a unit of this distributed work, allowing millions of people and machines to work in parallel.

Definition of a Task

In the MaxTask framework, a Task is a request for a specific type of research to be performed on a specific piece of evidence. Tasks are not free-form requests but structured units of work that can include: * Cost Caps: Limits on spending (e.g., preventing an AI from spending $50 on a $5 problem). * Deadlines: Timeframes for completion. * Dependencies: Requirements that a task must wait for another task to finish. * Escalation and Review: Procedures for handling failed tasks or auditing results.

The Standardized Task Types

The MaxTask schema currently recognizes ten specific task types that drive the downstream requirements for payloads and acceptance criteria:

Task Type Purpose
extract_record Extracting genealogical data from a MaxRecord.
validate_record Verifying the accuracy of an extraction.
merge_person Determining if two MaxPerson records represent the same individual.
judge_review Human or AI review of a specific research question.
resolve_conflict Resolving contradictory assertions in the data.
ingest_to_qdrant Embedding and indexing a record into a vector database.
build_tree Constructing a family tree segment from available evidence.
fetch_internet_archive Retrieving a document from the Internet Archive.
cost_report Summarizing API costs for a specific project.
other Miscellaneous tasks not covered by the above categories.

Quality Control and Evidence Mechanisms

Structured Results and Step Verdicts

A task result is a structured object rather than a free-form text blob. Every action taken during a task (searches, lookups, verifications) is documented in a steps[] array. Each step is assigned a verdict using a standardized vocabulary:

  • Key Finding (Green): The information is confirmed.
  • Usable (Blue): A useful lead has been identified.
  • Inconclusive (Amber): The results are mixed.
  • Ruled Out (Red): The hypothesis has been definitively disproved. This is considered a valuable "disproof" that changes the probability landscape.
  • Dead End (Grey): The source was searched, but nothing was found.
  • Infra (Grey): Relates to infrastructure tasks.

Acceptance Criteria and Evidence

  • acceptance_criteria[]: Objective, checkable conditions that define a complete result. They ensure results are comparable, auditable, and consistent.
  • evidence_required[]: An anti-hallucination mechanism. It requires workers to provide proof, such as source URLs, exact queries used, and citations. This is critical for AI workers to ensure they are grounding their results in actual records rather than confabulating.

The Review Gate

No task result is automatically accepted. The review{} object requires an independent reviewer (different from the contributor) to provide a review_verdict (accepted, rework, or rejected). A specific rejection reason, fabricated_or_hallucinated, is used to flag AI results that cannot be grounded in actual sources.

Privacy and Economics

The Privacy Gate

The contributor_eligibility field acts as a normative privacy gate. * first_party_only: The default (fail-closed) setting. Any task concerning a living person must use this setting. These tasks are never dispatched to external volunteers or third-party AI services. * any_contributor: Allows the task to be dispatched to external contributors.

Economic Neutrality

MaxTask does not include payment fields within the core standard. This maintains business model neutrality, allowing various platforms to use their own economic structures (e.g., paying volunteers vs. using unpaid contributors). Economic data is instead placed in the extensions{} namespace.

Comprehensive Glossary of Terms

Schema Field Names

  • acceptance_criteria[]: A list of objective, checkable conditions that must be met for a task result to be considered complete and acceptable.
  • contributor_eligibility: A field defining who is allowed to perform a task; serves as the primary privacy gate.
  • evidence_required[]: A field specifying the proof (URLs, citations, queries) a worker must provide to support their findings.
  • extensions{}: An open namespace for implementation-specific fields, such as payment or economic data, which are not part of the core standard.
  • review{}: An object containing data regarding the independent audit of a task result.
  • review_verdict: The status of a review, categorized as accepted, rework, or rejected.
  • rejection_reason: A field specifying why a task result was not accepted (e.g., fabricated_or_hallucinated or privacy_violation).
  • steps[]: An array documenting each individual action (search, lookup, etc.) taken during the execution of a task.
  • task_type: A field defining the nature of the research request, which determines the required payload and acceptance criteria.

Task Types

  • build_tree: The process of constructing a segment of a family tree based on evidence.
  • cost_report: A task that summarizes the API expenses associated with a project.
  • extract_record: The act of pulling structured genealogical data from a MaxRecord.
  • fetch_internet_archive: The retrieval of documents from the Internet Archive digital library.
  • ingest_to_qdrant: The process of embedding and indexing a record into a vector database for searchability.
  • judge_review: A task involving a human or AI assessment of a specific research question.
  • merge_person: The analytical task of determining if two separate person records represent the same individual.
  • resolve_conflict: The process of reconciling two or more contradictory assertions or data points.
  • validate_record: The verification process to ensure that data extracted from a record is accurate.

Verdict Vocabulary

  • dead_end (Grey): Indicates a source was searched, but no relevant information was found.
  • inconclusive (Amber): Indicates that the results of a step are mixed or unclear.
  • infra (Grey): A verdict assigned to infrastructure-related tasks rather than research findings.
  • key_finding (Green): A verdict indicating that a research point has been confirmed.
  • ruled_out (Red): A verdict indicating that a hypothesis or assertion has been definitively disproved.
  • usable (Blue): Indicates that a search provided a useful lead, even if not a final confirmation.

Genealogical and Technical Concepts

  • Any Contributor: A setting in contributor_eligibility that allows a task to be sent to external researchers or AI services.
  • Confabulation/Hallucination: A phenomenon where AI models generate plausible-sounding but false or ungrounded information.
  • Distributed Research: A work model where tasks are spread across many parallel contributors (human and machine) rather than one central authority.
  • Fail-Closed: A security/privacy design where the default setting is the most restrictive (first_party_only).
  • First Party Only: A setting in contributor_eligibility ensuring only the platform’s internal resources/personnel handle the task; mandatory for research on living persons.
  • MaxPerson: A structured record representing the interpretation of an individual’s identity and history.
  • MaxRecord: A structured representation of a piece of historical evidence or document.
  • MaxTask: The standardized format for describing a unit of work in distributed genealogical research.
  • Orchestrator/Task Dispatcher: A system or entity that reads MaxTask requests and assigns them to appropriate contributors.
  • Vector Database: A technical system (e.g., Qdrant) used for indexing and searching data through embeddings.
