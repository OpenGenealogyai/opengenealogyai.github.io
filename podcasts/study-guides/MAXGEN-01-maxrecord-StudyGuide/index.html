MaxRecord: The Evidence Layer of MAXGEN

This study guide serves as a comprehensive resource for understanding the MaxRecord schema, a foundational component of the MAXGEN system. MaxRecord is designed to capture genealogical evidence with absolute fidelity, prioritizing the preservation of historical data exactly as it was recorded, rather than as modern researchers might interpret it.

Key Concepts and Principles

The Distinction Between Evidence and Interpretation

The fundamental design principle of MaxRecord is the strict separation of evidence from interpretation. * MaxRecord (Evidence): Captures a document exactly as found. If a clerk misspells a name (e.g., "Makeswell" instead of "Maxwell"), MaxRecord preserves the misspelling. This "evidence layer" ensures that the original data is never lost or "fixed" by well-meaning indexers. * MaxPerson (Interpretation): This is where the determination is made that "Makeswell" refers to a specific individual named "Maxwell." Interpretations are tracked with confidence scores, allowing the system to remain scientific and errors to remain recoverable.

The Scientific System vs. Bad Databases

A "bad database" normalizes data by correcting historical spellings or dates to modern standards. This results in the loss of original text and causes AI models to be trained on "corrected" rather than "real" data, compounding errors over time. A scientific system, like MAXGEN, preserves the original evidence, allowing future researchers or improved AI models to re-examine the source.

Data Fidelity in Dates and Places

  • Range-Based Dates: MaxRecord uses a four-field range system (year_min, year_max, month, day) to represent historical uncertainty. Storing a precise date when a document is vague (e.g., "Spring 1847") is considered a falsehood; a range represents the historical truth.
  • Place Preservation: The system uses a place_as_written field to record exactly what the document states, which is then paired with optional normalized fields for modern geographic resolution.

The MAXGEN Pipeline

  1. Digitization: A document is scanned (e.g., a TIFF image).
  2. Transcription: MaxRecognition uses AI to produce a word-by-word transcription with confidence scores.
  3. Creation: A MaxRecord is generated, containing metadata, transcriptions, and the names of all persons mentioned.
  4. Tasking: A MaxTask is created to extract genealogical data.
  5. Interpretation: An agent identifies individuals and creates or updates MaxPerson records, linking them back to the original source_record_id.

Short-Answer Practice Questions

  1. What is the primary purpose of the record_id field? The record_id is a unique UUID used as a stable identifier to cite a specific document as the basis for a genealogical claim. It never changes, even if a record is retracted.

  2. Why does MaxRecord store every person mentioned in a document rather than just the "principal" person? Storing all names in the persons_mentioned[] array preserves the social network of the document, including witnesses, neighbors, and informants who might otherwise be lost but are vital for deep research.

  3. How does the is_living_flag affect data privacy? If the is_living_flag is set to true, the record is automatically classified as tier2-private, regardless of other settings. This prevents the data of living people from appearing in public datasets.

  4. What happens to a record if it is discovered to be a forgery? The record is not deleted; it is retracted. It retains its ID and history, but a retraction timestamp is added to ensure the audit trail remains intact.

  5. Describe how MaxRecord handles a document dated only as "Spring 1847." The system uses year_min: 1847 and year_max: 1847, while omitting the month and day fields because a specific date cannot be precisely determined.

  2. The Social Network of Records: Discuss the significance of the persons_mentioned[] array. How does capturing witnesses, officiants, and neighbors change the way a genealogist might reconstruct a community compared to traditional "principal-only" indexing?
