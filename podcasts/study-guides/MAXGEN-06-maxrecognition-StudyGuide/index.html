MaxRecognition Study Guide: Capturing the Provenance of Transcription

This study guide provides a comprehensive overview of the MaxRecognition schema, a specialized framework within the MAXGEN standard designed to document the process of machine and human transcription of historical documents.

I. Core Concepts and Architecture

The Handwriting Problem

The primary driver for MaxRecognition is the "handwriting problem" in genealogy. Historical records, particularly German church registers from the 18th and 19th centuries, are often written in scripts such as Kurrent. This cursive, angular script is significantly different from modern handwriting (e.g., the letter "e" does not resemble a modern "e"). Other historical scripts include Secretary Hand, Court Hand, Greek and Hebrew scribal scripts, Arabic nastaliq, and Cyrillic ustav. MaxRecognition provides a verifiable, citable, and improvable provenance record for transcribing these opaque texts.

The "One Schema" Philosophy

MaxRecognition uses a single schema to handle both Optical Character Recognition (OCR) for printed text and Handwritten Text Recognition (HTR). While the problems differ, they share over 90% of the same fields. * recognition_type: The discriminator field used to distinguish between ocr and htr. * HTR-Specific Fields: script_type (e.g., kurrent, gothic_fraktur) and approx_century. * OCR-Specific Fields: font_type and scan_dpi.

Contributor Architecture

The contributor{} object is a polymorphic structure that changes based on the contributor_type (either human or ai).

1. Human Contributors

Human transcription quality is assessed through specialized fields that act as reputation and competence signals: * Specialty Fields: specialty_scripts[], specialty_languages[], and specialty_regions[]. * Reputation Signal: records_transcribed tracks the volume of work. A high volume (e.g., 50,000 records) without documented errors increases the confidence weight of that contributor’s transcriptions. * Credentials: credentials[] and training_notes provide further context on the contributor's expertise.

2. AI Contributors

For machine-led recognition, the schema tracks the specific methodology used: * Model Identification: model_name, model_version, and model_provider. * model_type: Categorizes the architecture (e.g., llm_vision, transformer, cnn_lstm, rule_based, hybrid). * prompt{}: Captures the exact system and user prompts (including temperature) for LLM-based runs. This ensures the transcription is reproducible and auditable. * fine_tuning{}: Records the base model and the specific training data used (e.g., a model fine-tuned on 100,000 Württemberg church records).

II. Word-Level Analysis and Confidence

The word_confidences[] array is the most detailed component of the schema, providing a per-word breakdown of the transcription.

Reading Types

MaxRecognition distinguishes how a word was produced using the reading_type field:

Reading Type Description
observed Directly read from the image.
inferred_context Logically filled in based on surrounding entries in the document.
inferred_pattern Filled in based on known naming conventions.
partial_read Some characters are legible; the remainder are inferred.

Inference and Transparency

Unlike traditional standards that use [illegible], MaxRecognition encourages transcribers to make educated guesses while labeling them as inferences. * inference_notes: Required for any reading type other than observed. It details the reasoning (e.g., "Characters 1-2 read 'Ma'... five other surname entries on this page read Maxwell... Inference: Maxwell"). * Confidence Scores: Inferred readings are assigned lower confidence scores (e.g., 0.78) than observed readings.

III. Data Integration and Quality

Cross-Run Consensus

Multiple independent transcriptions of the same image are linked via a consensus_group_id. * is_independent: A boolean indicating if the run was performed "blind" to others. * Logic: The schema stores the evidence; a separate matching engine computes the consensus. Agreement between independent runs raises confidence, while disagreement flags the record for human review.

Searchable Variants

The searchable_variants[] field stores all potential spellings to ensure findability. This differs from MaxName: * MaxName: Handles semantic equivalence (e.g., "Maxwell" and "Makeswell" are the same name). * searchable_variants[]: Handles visual uncertainty (e.g., the transcriber is unsure if the ink says "Maxwell" or "Maxweil").

Quality Metrics

When "ground truth" (a definitive manual transcription) is available, the schema stores: * CER (Character Error Rate): Standard metric for character-level accuracy. * WER (Word Error Rate): Standard metric for word-level accuracy. These metrics help build a "quality map" to dispatch difficult documents to the most capable models or humans.

IV. Short-Answer Practice Questions

  1. What is the primary purpose of the recognition_type field? It serves as the discriminator to identify whether the transcription record was produced via OCR (printed) or HTR (handwritten).
  2. Identify three specific historical scripts mentioned as challenges for researchers. Kurrent, Secretary Hand, and Arabic nastaliq (also Court Hand, Greek/Hebrew scribal scripts, and Cyrillic ustav).
  3. Why is the prompt{} field considered essential for LLM-based transcriptions? It makes the transcription reproducible and auditable by documenting the exact instructions given to the AI, such as whether it was told to transcribe exactly or to normalize the text.
  4. What is the difference between inferred_context and inferred_pattern? inferred_context relies on surrounding document entries, while inferred_pattern relies on known naming conventions.
  5. How does the schema handle a word that is only partially legible? It uses the reading_type of partial_read, requires inference_notes to explain the logic, and assigns a lower confidence score.
  6. What does a records_transcribed value of 50,000 signify in a human contributor's profile? It serves as a reputation signal of demonstrated competence, which feeds into the confidence weighting of their transcriptions.
  7. What is the function of the consensus_group_id? It links multiple independent transcriptions of the same document image so they can be compared for agreement or disagreement.
  8. How do searchable_variants[] improve searchability in the face of uncertainty? They store alternative readings for uncertain words (e.g., "Maxwell" and "Maxweil"), allowing the record to be found regardless of which spelling the searcher uses.
  9. Name the two standard metrics used by MaxRecognition to evaluate model quality against ground truth. Character Error Rate (CER) and Word Error Rate (WER).
  10. What is the core design principle regarding computation in MAXGEN? "Store evidence, compute elsewhere." The schema stores the raw data and evidence, while external engines perform calculations like consensus or confidence weighting.

VI. Glossary of Important Terms

  • approx_century: A field used in HTR records to help route documents to the correct chronological AI model.
  • character_error_rate (CER): A metric used to evaluate the accuracy of a transcription at the character level against a known "ground truth."
  • cnn_lstm: A type of AI model architecture (Convolutional Neural Network / Long Short-Term Memory) used for recognition.
  • consensus_group_id: A unique identifier used to group different transcription runs of the same document for comparison.
  • contributor{}: The schema object containing data about the entity (human or AI) that performed the transcription.
  • credentials[]: A list of professional certifications or qualifications held by a human contributor (e.g., CG-certified).
  • fine_tuning{}: An object capturing details about the specific training an AI model received, including the base model and the training dataset.
  • htr (Handwritten Text Recognition): The process of using machines or humans to transcribe handwritten text.
  • inferred_context: A reading_type where a word is identified based on the logic of surrounding entries.
  • inferred_pattern: A reading_type where a word is identified based on known naming or cultural conventions.
  • inference_notes: A required text field for non-observed readings that explains the reasoning behind a transcribed word.
  • is_independent: A flag indicating if a transcription run was performed without knowledge of other runs.
  • Kurrent: A cursive, angular German script used from the 16th to the mid-20th century; a primary target for HTR in genealogy.
  • MaxName: A related schema that manages the semantic equivalence of names (e.g., recognizing different spellings as the same entity).
  • ocr (Optical Character Recognition): The process of transcribing printed text.
  • partial_read: A reading_type used when only some characters are legible and the rest must be inferred.
  • prompt{}: An object in AI contributors that stores the exact system and user instructions used in LLM-based recognition.
  • reading_type: A field classifying how a specific word was determined (observed vs. various types of inference).
  • recognition_type: The discriminator field indicating if a record is ocr or htr.
  • records_transcribed: A reputation signal for human contributors tracking the total number of records they have processed.
  • searchable_variants[]: A field containing all potential versions of a word to ensure it can be found in a search, despite transcription uncertainty.
  • specialty_scripts[]: A human contributor field listing specific handwriting styles they are trained to read (e.g., Kurrent, Secretary Hand).
  • word_confidences[]: An array providing a granular, per-word breakdown of the transcription, including confidence scores and offsets.
  • word_error_rate (WER): A metric used to evaluate the accuracy of a transcription at the word level against a known "ground truth."
