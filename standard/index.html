The Maxwell Genealogy Standard · v2.0 · CC0

The Standard

MAXGEN is a language for describing family-history evidence. It is not a product, not a database, and not a website. It is a set of eleven record schemas (written rule-sets for data records), plus one shared file of building blocks, that any program can read and write, so that research done in one place can be understood everywhere else. This page explains the standard in plain English. The exact rules live in the schema files.

1. What is MAXGEN?

MAXGEN is an open JSON standard for representing genealogical evidence and the people it describes in a way that is honest about uncertainty.

Some or most genealogy formats today can hold one answer per fact. When two records disagree about who someone's father was, one has to win and the other is set aside. MAXGEN takes a different approach: every fact is a claim that cites its evidence and carries a confidence score. Nothing is overwritten. Several possible parents can coexist. Conflicting birth years both survive. The uncertainty is the data.

Five ideas define it:

  1. Uncertainty is data. Every claim carries a 0-to-1 confidence score (section 6). A date keeps the document's own words plus what they mean (about, before, between, worked out from an age), and its earliest and latest possible days are worked out whenever it is read, because a census age is a guess and "spring 1847" is not a day.
  2. Evidence and interpretation are kept apart. MaxRecord holds what a document says, exactly as written. MaxPerson and MaxRelationship hold what we think it means: who the people were and how they were related. You can change your mind about a person without touching the document.
  3. Nothing is ever deleted. A wrong claim is retracted with a reason and stays visible as withdrawn, and a wrong merge can be undone. A contested claim is marked disputed, not removed. Every claim names the task that produced it, and every task records who did the work.
  4. Privacy is built into the schema. Every document that names a person must say whether that person may be alive, and when unsure the answer is yes. Anything about a person who may be alive is locked to the most restrictive licence, tier2-private, and privacy is worked out again whenever the data is read, never from a stored copy. DNA records are always private and never hold raw genetic data.
  5. Extensions are the escape hatch. Each schema is closed, so validation is exact, but every schema has an extensions{} object that accepts anything. You never need to fork.

The core principle: a claim with low confidence and a real source is worth more than a certain-looking answer with no source. MAXGEN never invents. When the document is unclear, the confidence score says so.

2. How MAXGEN differs from GEDCOM

GEDCOM is the 1984 file format that most genealogy programs can import and export. It is one of the great successes of the field: forty years of trees have moved between programs because of it, and GEDCOM 7 continues to modernise it. MAXGEN is not a replacement for exchanging finished trees. It is a companion for exchanging the evidence and reasoning behind a tree, a job GEDCOM was never asked to do. The two are meant to work together.

GEDCOM MAXGEN
Format Custom plain text (.ged) JSON, validated by JSON Schema
Relationships A child can be linked to more than one family, with a pedigree type (PEDI) Every family link stored once, in MaxRelationship; a child can belong to several parent sets (birth, adoptive, step, foster and more), and a competing candidate is kept until a written proof settles it
Uncertainty A quality rating on a citation (QUAY) The point of the design: a confidence on every claim
Source for each fact Optional Required on every claim: exactly one piece of evidence
Dates A date value, with an optional phrase (PHRASE) The words as written, plus what they mean (about, before, between, worked out from an age); earliest and latest days are worked out when read
Conflicts A child's family link can be marked challenged or disproven (STAT) Competing claims are kept side by side, and each claim's status says where it stands (proposed, proven, disputed, disproven, retracted)
Living people A restriction marker (RESN) Every document that names a person must say whether they may be alive; if so, the schema locks it private
Transcription provenance None MaxRecognition, down to word-level confidence; on a MaxRecord, the chain of copies back to the original
Name variants across languages Translations of a name (TRAN) MaxName: variants and cross-language cognates, each citing evidence
AI readiness Predates it Structured evidence on every claim, quotes checked against the document's words, and a fabricated_or_hallucinated rejection reason for reviews in MaxTask
Control A steering committee Public domain (CC0); anyone may use, fork, or extend

The MAXGEN 2.0 specification gives every GEDCOM 7 construct a home, so a GEDCOM import need not lose anything on the way in: CENS, EMIG, IMMI, NATU, PROB and WILL become events of the matching type, RESI and OCCU become residence and occupation facts, and a tag with no field of its own is kept word for word in extensions. It maps GEDCOM X, Gramps and RootsMagic the same way. The importers that follow this mapping are not published yet.

3. The eleven record schemas

A schema is a written rule-set that says exactly what fields a record has and what each one means. MAXGEN began with three record types, grew to seven during the 1.x releases, and in 2.0 has eleven record schemas plus one shared building-blocks file, each covering one layer of the research process. Each record schema has its own page.

Schema What one document is Page
MaxRecord One document, copied word for word MaxRecord
MaxPerson One person, as a researched conclusion (holds no family links) MaxPerson
MaxRelationship One family link, stored once: a family, a parent_child pair or an association MaxRelationship
MaxTask One piece of work: research, an import, a review or a proof MaxTask
MaxSource One work, collection or repository MaxSource
MaxPlace One real place with its history MaxPlace
MaxGroup One historical group (its own facts only; no member list) MaxGroup
MaxEvent The identity of one occasion (thin) MaxEvent
MaxName Knowledge about one name MaxName
MaxRecognition One machine reading of an image MaxRecognition
MaxDNA One person's DNA test and its matches (always private) MaxDNA

The twelfth file, maxgen-defs.schema.json, is not a record type: no document is valid against it. It holds the parts every record schema uses: ids and small types, the date block, the place block, the evidence link (the part of every claim that says which evidence it rests on), the quote check, saved copies, regions and media pointers, roles and event types, the claim header, the common and lifecycle fields, and the task provenance block.

Every claim, in every schema, cites exactly one piece of evidence: a document (a MaxRecord), an imported tree file, DNA matches, an accepted proof, or a recorded search. Every claim also names the MaxTask that produced it.

A MaxRecord needs no other document to be valid, only the shared file every schema uses, so an archive that only wants to publish its documents, copied word for word, can start with MaxRecord alone.

Sections 4 to 7 are the rules that MAXGEN's reference agent kit follows, copied word for word from its RULES.md. Where they mention "the kit", "the helper", the Orchestrator or the person's page, they mean that kit.

4. Who decides

The kit cannot tell who is typing. A decision counts as the person's own only when the person makes it on their Probable Pedigree page (probable_pedigree.py). An answer the Orchestrator types for them (task ... --answer) is recorded as relayed. A relayed answer is enough for a merge, an adoption, lowering a living flag, and for "disputed" or "disproven". "Proven" needs the person's own decision on their page. So the person-page address is for the person's own hands: an agent never opens it, fetches it with a program or presses its buttons, and never reuses it after giving it to the person.

The person's own choices are theirs alone (R-33, R-34). Who is held back from sharing, what is shared, which people are locked, and their bookmarks, tags and saved lists live in the tree's owner tables. Only the person changes them, in their own Probable Pedigree, which holds the tree's owner key; you never ask for that key, never look for it and never write those tables. Never change a person the owner has locked, nor a family link of theirs: the helper refuses it (R-34). If a locked person needs a change, tell the person through the Orchestrator and let them unlock it. The one thing you may record there is how many records a website holds for a person (an outside_holdings mark), because that is a finding, not a choice.

Retract, never delete. Nothing is removed from the database: a wrong claim, document or link is withdrawn with retract and a reason, and stays visible as withdrawn. Every change you make is also logged in the tree itself, with your session, so the person can see it and undo it.

5. Evidence standards

MAXGEN's confidence number measures how faithfully a fact was read from its source — extraction confidence — not how good the source is. A clearly printed name copied from a clearly printed page is 0.9 even if the page is a hearsay family tree. How much the source is worth is a separate question, answered for every MaxRecord by its source_classification, a required field of the record:

  • source: original (the document made at the time — a register page, a certificate, a stone — or an image of it), derivative (a copy, index, transcription or abstract made from an original; it must say what it was copied from, in copied_from), authored (someone's written conclusions — a compiled history, an online tree, the family's own account), or undetermined (only for a citation in an imported tree that nobody has examined yet).
  • information: primary (the informant was present at the event), secondary (the informant heard it from someone else, or wrote it long afterwards), or undetermined (a census row, where nobody knows who answered the door). A record that holds several events judges this per event: a death certificate's informant is first-hand for the death and second-hand for the birth.

Every claim also says what kind of evidence it is: direct (the words state it), indirect (it is worked out — a birth year from an age), or negative (someone missing from a complete list).

These three sets of words follow the evidence analysis process map in Elizabeth Shown Mills's Evidence Explained (QuickLesson 17), described here in our own words.

A claim is called proven only when it meets the Genealogical Proof Standard (published by the Board for Certification of Genealogists) — a reasonably exhaustive search, complete citations, analysis and correlation of the evidence, resolution of any conflicts, and a written conclusion. In this kit that written conclusion is a proof on a resolve_conflict task (or, for a merge, a merge_person task), and the status "proven" is set only when the person accepts that proof on their own page ("Who decides", above). Until then it is a claim with a confidence, and the kit says so. An import can mark a claim disputed or disproven, never proven.

6. The confidence score

confidence is a number from 0 to 1. It is how strongly this document supports this claim, not how sure you feel. A census row names a person: 0.8 to 0.9. A gravestone with a worn date: 0.5 to 0.7. A family tree on a website with no citations: never above 0.4, and never alone. It is extraction confidence; the weight of the evidence is the source classification above.

Deliberate exception, enforced by the helper: a claim whose evidence is not reliable — the family's own account in the interview, something the person typed on their page, an online tree, an imported file, a compiled work, a citation nobody has examined — is capped at 0.4 and never stands alone: it never counts as independent evidence, never supports a proof, and never shows that someone has died. Pages label it by where it came from ("From the family's account", "From a family tree", "From an imported file", "Added by you"). The cap lifts only when a new claim cites a reliable document.

Why this matters for AI: models that read handwriting, match records and draft leads need an honest confidence on every claim, a source they can be checked against, and the losing candidates as well as the winner. That is why every claim cites exactly one piece of evidence (for a document: the record, the line holding the words, the exact quote, and whether the evidence is direct, indirect or negative), why claims are never overwritten, and why a MaxTask can list the evidence its worker must hand back (addresses, exact queries, records saved) and its search log keeps every step, including the searches that found nothing.

7. Who may be living

Treat a person as possibly living unless at least one of these holds: a reliable document states their death, burial or cremation (a later record calling their husband or wife a widow or widower also counts; being missing from a list never does); their latest possible birth year is 110 or more years ago; a marriage of theirs is 90 or more years ago; or a birth child of theirs was born 90 or more years ago (always counting the latest year the date allows). A step-child, ward, foster child or adopted child does not count. If none of these holds — for example, when the birth year is unknown and no death is on file — assume living. Never infer "deceased" from silence — not finding a death record is not a death record.

Only reliable evidence counts here: a document classified original or derivative, or a proof whose latest review is accepted. The family's own account, an online tree, an imported file and a note typed on the person page never show that anyone has died. So a grandparent known only from the interview stays living until a document shows otherwise.

Set is_living explicitly on every MaxPerson and every person named in a record, true or false. The helper refuses a file that omits it. The helper and every page work this out again every time they read; a stored false never makes anyone shareable. A stored true is a floor: it stays until the person agrees to lower it (ORCHESTRATOR, "When the documents show someone has died"). What the person tells you about living relatives stays tier2-private and is used only for their own research — never published, exported or sent anywhere.

8. JSON examples

A MaxPerson is one person as a researched conclusion. Every fact about the person is a claim, kept in a list (the files name these lists ..._assertions), and every claim cites exactly one piece of evidence and carries a confidence. A MaxPerson holds no family links: parents, children and partners live in MaxRelationship, where each link is stored once.

This made-up example takes three claims from one entry on a census page: a name, an occupation, and a birth year worked out from an age. 8b. Contributor attribution

Credit travels with the data, not in a site footer. A MaxRecord can carry an attribution{} object crediting the human who photographed, transcribed or compiled it (contributor_name, contributor_url, contributor_role) and the organisation the document came from (source_organization, source_organization_url); a MaxSource and a MaxRecognition can carry one too. Every claim names the task that produced it (task_id), and each MaxTask records once, in its provenance block, who did the work: a person, an AI model (named), an OCR or handwriting reader, an importer, and so on. For a MaxRecognition, which holds one machine reading of an image, who or what made the reading is recorded the same way, on the task that produced it. A credit never makes a document count as naming a living person. In a copy meant for sharing, a credit is kept when it is already public or when the licence requires it (CC-BY, CC-BY-SA), exactly as the licence holder gave it; other credits, such as a name typed in as transcriber or photographer, are left out.

9. File format

A MAXGEN document is one JSON file of one kind: one MaxPerson, one MaxRecord, and so on. A document's kind is the first of these keys found at its top level: dna_id, recognition_id, name_id, relationship_id, occurrence_id, place_id, group_id, record_id, task_id, source_id, person_id. (MaxDNA also carries person_id, so the order matters.)

10. Versioning

All twelve files share one version number; the current release is v2.0. Every document carries a schema_version stamp. The schemas accept any "2.<n>" stamp ("2.0", "2.1" and so on, but not "1.13"), and a file keeps the stamp it was written with.

Within 2.x, every change is additive: a new optional property, a new value in a list, a new shared definition in maxgen-defs.schema.json, a new record schema file, or a loosened limit. Never: removing or renaming a property, making an optional property required, narrowing a pattern, range or list, or changing what a value means. A file valid under 2.0 stays valid under every 2.x. Breaking changes wait for a new major version, with a new address space; the governance page describes how changes are proposed and decided.

The 1.x files stay archived at /schemas/maxgen/v1/, with a frozen copy of every 1.x release, and their rules are unchanged. MAXGEN 2.0 does not read 1.x files, and there is no migration from 1.x to 2.0. Every release, with permanent download links, is on the versions page, and the full history is in the changelog.

11. Why JSON?

  • Human-readable and machine-readable. No proprietary parser required; you can open a record in a text editor.
  • Supported everywhere. Every modern programming language reads and writes JSON natively.
  • Validated by JSON Schema 2020-12. Structure is enforced at the moment a record is written, not discovered later.
  • Embeddable. A MAXGEN record can be fed to an AI embedding model without conversion, which is what makes meaning-based search possible.
  • Version-control friendly. Records can be diffed, reviewed and merged exactly like source code, so a community can review each other's work.
  • No lock-in. Export your data at any time with zero loss.

12. How to contribute

Ask a question, report a problem, propose a change, or build something on the standard. You do not need permission for any of it. The contribute page has the details; the short version is: open a GitHub Issue using the Schema Change Proposal template (problem, proposed change, backward compatibility, examples) or write to maxgen@opengenealogyai.org.

13. Governance

MAXGEN is public domain, so anyone can use it, fork it, or extend it. What is stewarded is the canonical version: the one set of files at this address that may be called MAXGEN. Today the author, Garlon Maxwell, is the steward, with a public proposal process (7-day comment for minor changes, 30-day for major) and written decisions. A published charter describes the advisory board that takes over major-version decisions once there are independent adopters, including how it can overrule the steward. What the steward never controls: your forks, your extensions, your tools, or your records. Read the full governance page.

"MAXGEN is open because anyone can use it and build on it. It is not open to fragmentation. One canonical version. One steward. Every change earns its place in the record." — Garlon Maxwell
