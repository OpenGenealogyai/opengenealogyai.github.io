# Frequently asked questions

Grouped by who is asking. If your question is not here, [ask it](/contribute/) and it may be added.

## I'm a genealogist

<details><summary>What is MAXGEN in one sentence?</summary><p>A free, public-domain way of writing down family-history evidence so that every fact keeps its source and an honest confidence score, and conflicting evidence is kept rather than deleted.</p></details>

<details><summary>Do I need to be technical to benefit from it?</summary><p>No. MAXGEN is what software uses underneath. What you notice is the behaviour: a tree that keeps two possible fathers, each with its evidence, instead of silently picking one, a search that finds "Quilfether" when you typed "Quillfeather", and a document that is never "corrected" away from what the clerk wrote.</p></details>

<details><summary>Why keep several possible parents? Isn't the job to find the right one?</summary><p>Yes, and keeping the candidates is how you get there honestly. Real research often ends at "probably this man, possibly that one". A format that can hold only one answer has no room for the uncertainty a good genealogist would write in the margin. MAXGEN stores the margin note as data: a competing set of parents is kept as its own link, with its own evidence, marked as not yet settled until a written proof settles it, so a new record adds evidence instead of overturning a false certainty.</p></details>

<details><summary>What do the confidence numbers mean?</summary><p>A confidence is a number from 0 to 1. It is how strongly <em>this document</em> supports <em>this claim</em>, not how sure you feel. A census row names a person: 0.8 to 0.9. A gravestone with a worn date: 0.5 to 0.7. A family tree on a website with no citations: never above 0.4, and never alone. It measures how faithfully a fact was read from its source; how much the source itself is worth is recorded separately, on the document, as its source classification. A claim whose evidence is not reliable (the family's own account, an online tree, an imported file) is capped at 0.4 and never counts as independent evidence. The full rule is on <a href="/standard/#5-the-confidence-score">the standard page</a>.</p></details>

<details><summary>How does it handle a document that gets the spelling wrong?</summary><p>The document record (MaxRecord) keeps the spelling exactly as written. The interpretation ("that is the Quillfeather family") lives separately, as a claim with its own evidence and confidence. And the name record (MaxName) can state, citing evidence, that Quilfether is a spelling variant of Quillfeather used between 1700 and 1800, so searches find it.</p></details>

<details><summary>Will my existing GEDCOM files work?</summary><p>MAXGEN is not a replacement for GEDCOM as a way to move a finished tree between programs. It is a way to exchange the evidence and reasoning behind a tree. MAXGEN 2.0 gives every GEDCOM 7 construct a place to land, so a tool that brings your file into MAXGEN need not lose anything: each fact arrives as a claim that remembers it came from your tree, and it serves as a lead (with a confidence of at most 0.4) until a document confirms it.</p></details>

<details><summary>What about living relatives?</summary><p>Every document that names a person must say whether that person may be alive; a document that leaves it out is refused, and when in doubt the answer is "living". Anything about a person who may be alive is <code>tier2-private</code> (kept private), and whether someone may be living is worked out again whenever the data is read, so a stored "no" never makes anyone shareable. DNA records are always private and can never hold raw genetic data. Research tasks about living people must stay first-party: they can never be opened to outside contributors. These are rules of the standard, not settings an application can turn off.</p></details>

<details><summary>Can AI make things up in a MAXGEN record?</summary><p>It can try, and the standard is built to catch it. Every claim must cite exactly one piece of evidence; a claim drawn from a document names the line it comes from and quotes the exact words (or marks the area of the image), and quotes are checked against the document's words. A research task can list the evidence its worker must hand back (addresses, exact queries, records saved), and its search log keeps every step, including the searches that found nothing. Reviews are separate tasks that are never overwritten, "fabricated or hallucinated" is a formal rejection reason, and in 2.0 nothing can be marked proven without a human's review of a written proof.</p></details>

<details><summary>Does it cost anything?</summary><p>No. The standard is dedicated to the public domain (CC0): use it for anything, including commercial products, with no conditions. The schemas, the specification, and the podcasts on this site cost nothing.</p></details>

## I'm a developer

<details><summary>What format is it?</summary><p>JSON, described by JSON Schema (draft 2020-12). Twelve schema files, all at the same version number: eleven record schemas, one for each kind of document, and one shared building-blocks file (<code>maxgen-defs.schema.json</code>) that the others point into. Each document is a plain JSON file of one kind; its kind is told by the first id key found at its top level (<code>dna_id</code>, <code>recognition_id</code>, <code>name_id</code> and so on, with <code>person_id</code> last).</p></details>

<details><summary>Where do I get the schema files?</summary><p>At their canonical addresses, which are also each file's <code>$id</code>: <code>https://opengenealogyai.org/schemas/maxgen/v2/&lt;file name&gt;</code>, for example <code>.../v2/person.schema.json</code>. A frozen copy of the 2.0 release lives under <code>/schemas/maxgen/v2.0/</code>, with a SHA-256 list, and the archived 1.x files stay at <code>/schemas/maxgen/v1/</code> and <code>/schemas/maxgen/v1.NN/</code>. Validators load all twelve files locally and never need to fetch an address over the network. See the <a href="/schemas/">schemas page</a> and <a href="/versions/">versions</a>.</p></details>

<details><summary>How do I validate a record?</summary><p>With any JSON Schema 2020-12 validator that can hold several schema files in one registry. Load all twelve files (the shared <code>maxgen-defs.schema.json</code> first) and do not let the validator fetch anything over the network. In Python that is <code>jsonschema</code> 4.18 or later with <code>referencing</code>; in JavaScript it is Ajv 2020, calling <code>ajv.addSchema()</code> with the shared file before compiling a record schema. The schema repository ships validators and made-up fixtures. Or paste a record into the <a href="/validator/">browser validator</a> on this site.</p></details>

<details><summary>Can I add my own fields?</summary><p>Yes, in <code>extensions{}</code>, which every record schema has (claims, mentions and other smaller items have one too) and which accepts anything. Apart from a few named places, every other object is closed: unknown field names are refused, so validation is exact, and the extensions object is open so you never need to fork. Your records stay valid across 2.x releases.</p></details>

<details><summary>Will an update break my records?</summary><p>Not within 2.x. Every change inside 2.x is additive: a new optional property, a new value in a list, a new shared definition, a new record schema file, or a loosened limit. Properties are never removed or renamed, optional ones never become required, patterns, ranges and lists are never narrowed, and what a value means never changes, so a file valid under 2.0 stays valid under every 2.x. The step from 1.x to 2.0 was different: it was a breaking change, 2.0 does not read 1.x files, and there is no migration. The 1.x files stay archived at their <code>/v1/</code> addresses with their rules unchanged. See the <a href="/changelog/">changelog</a>.</p></details>

<details><summary>Why is one version number shared by all twelve files?</summary><p>So there is never any doubt about which files go together: you adopt MAXGEN 2.x as a set. Every document carries a <code>schema_version</code> stamp, and the schemas accept any 2.x stamp, so a file keeps the stamp it was written with. From v1.3 to v1.13 every release re-stamped all seven files, even those that did not change; 2.x drops that ritual. Before v1.3 the schemas versioned independently, and the skew was confusing.</p></details>

<details><summary>Which fields are required?</summary><p>Few, deliberately, and each kind has its own. Every document needs its own id (<code>person_id</code>, <code>record_id</code> and so on) and <code>schema_version</code>, and every claim needs <code>evidence</code> and a <code>confidence</code>. Beyond that: a MaxPerson needs <code>is_living</code>, <code>redistribution_license</code> and at least one name claim (a placeholder person needs a <code>placeholder_label</code> instead); a MaxRecord needs <code>record_type</code>, <code>redistribution_license</code>, <code>is_living_flag</code>, <code>source_classification</code> and a way to find the document (a <code>source_url</code>, a saved copy, or a repository or source with a <code>locator</code>); a MaxRelationship needs <code>kind</code>, <code>is_living_flag</code> and <code>redistribution_license</code>, plus what its kind needs (a parent-child pair names the parent and the child); a MaxTask needs <code>task_type</code>, <code>status</code> and <code>is_living_flag</code>; a MaxSource needs <code>name</code>, <code>source_type</code>, <code>redistribution_license</code> and <code>is_living_flag</code>; a MaxPlace needs <code>redistribution_license</code> and at least one name claim; a MaxGroup needs <code>group_kind</code>, <code>is_living_flag</code>, <code>redistribution_license</code> and at least one name claim; a MaxEvent needs <code>event_type</code>, <code>origin</code>, <code>is_living_flag</code> and <code>redistribution_license</code>; a MaxName needs <code>canonical_form</code>, <code>name_type</code> and <code>redistribution_license</code>; a MaxRecognition needs <code>recognition_type</code>, <code>output_text</code>, <code>processed_at</code>, <code>is_living_flag</code>, <code>redistribution_license</code> and an <code>image_url</code> or <code>raw_record_id</code>; a MaxDNA needs <code>person_id</code>, <code>test_type</code>, <code>kit_source</code>, <code>is_living_flag</code>, <code>redistribution_license</code>, <code>raw_genotype_stored</code> and <code>consent_status</code>, and a <code>kit_id_hash</code> unless <code>kit_id_status</code> is <code>unknown</code>. Each schema page lists its fields with the required ones marked.</p></details>

<details><summary>What are the normative rules I must implement?</summary><p>The privacy rules are written into the schemas, so every valid document already obeys them. Every document that names a person must say whether that person may be alive (<code>is_living</code> on a MaxPerson and on each person a record names, <code>is_living_flag</code> on the other kinds that can name someone), and anything that may concern a living person must be <code>tier2-private</code>. A MaxDNA document is always <code>tier2-private</code>, <code>raw_genotype_stored</code> is always <code>false</code>, and no raw kit number is ever stored, only a keyed fingerprint. A saved DNA match list is always treated as living and private. A MaxTask about someone who may be living must keep <code>contributor_eligibility</code> at <code>first_party_only</code>. Two rules no schema can check complete the core: when unsure, a person counts as living, and whether someone may be living is worked out again every time the data is read, so a stored <code>false</code> is never permission to share. Rules a schema cannot express are stated in the field descriptions.</p></details>

<details><summary>How are confidence scores combined?</summary><p>They are not stored combined. Each claim keeps its own <code>confidence</code>, and no schema has a field for a computed score: anything worked out from the evidence, such as whether two sources are independent, is worked out when the data is read. Independence fails closed: two documents count as independent only when they are different originals with different informants, and a claim whose evidence is not reliable (an online tree, an imported file, the family's own account) never counts toward it. That is how one document copied twice is never counted twice.</p></details>

<details><summary>Is there an official library or package?</summary><p>Not yet. The schema repository contains JavaScript validators and made-up fixtures. Packages for npm and PyPI are planned after launch; if you publish one, tell us and it will be listed.</p></details>

<details><summary>How do I propose a change?</summary><p>Open a GitHub Issue with the problem, the proposed fields, the backward-compatibility answer and a before-and-after example. Minor changes get at least a week of open discussion, major ones a month, then a written decision. Details on the <a href="/governance/">governance page</a>.</p></details>

## I run an archive, library or society

<details><summary>What would adopting MAXGEN commit us to?</summary><p>Only to publishing records that validate against the public schemas and to honouring the privacy rules they carry. There is no membership, no fee, no agreement to sign, and no obligation to use the whole standard: an archive that only wants to publish its documents, copied word for word, can start with MaxRecord alone (plus the shared building-blocks file every schema uses).</p></details>

<details><summary>Who owns the standard?</summary><p>Nobody, legally: it is dedicated to the public domain under CC0, so anyone may use, copy, change and redistribute it with no conditions. The canonical version is stewarded by its author today, with a published path to an advisory board that includes an archive or library seat and can overrule the steward on major versions by a two-thirds vote. See <a href="/governance/">governance</a>.</p></details>

<details><summary>Can we keep our own catalogue numbers and internal fields?</summary><p>Yes. A MaxRecord says where the item sits in your own words (<code>repository_as_written</code>, <code>collection_as_written</code>) and in a structured <code>locator</code> (volume, page, line and so on), and <code>extensions{}</code> holds anything else. A MaxSource for a collection lists the repositories that hold it, with their call numbers and which one holds the original (<code>holdings</code>), and its <code>access</code> block records how you want to be reached, including politeness and rate-limit notes for automated searching.</p></details>

<details><summary>How does it handle attribution and licensing of our records?</summary><p>Every record declares its own licence: <code>CC0</code>, <code>public-domain</code>, <code>CC-BY</code>, <code>CC-BY-SA</code>, <code>all_rights_reserved</code>, or <code>tier2-private</code> (kept private). A record can also carry an <code>attribution{}</code> object for the credit line your licence requires, and when a licence requires credit, a copy made for sharing keeps it exactly as you gave it. The standard itself is public domain; your data stays under whatever terms you set.</p></details>

<details><summary>We have transcriptions made by volunteers over decades. Is that work preserved?</summary><p>Yes. A volunteer's transcription is kept as a MaxRecord: the words exactly as transcribed, classified as a <code>derivative</code> (a copy made from an original) that says what it was copied from, with the volunteer credited by name and role (transcriber, indexer, translator and so on) in its <code>attribution{}</code>. Machine readings (OCR of print, HTR of handwriting) get a MaxRecognition, which can keep a confidence for every word and note which words were read from the image and which were inferred. Either way the work becomes citable instead of being flattened into a text field.</p></details>

## About the project

<details><summary>Why is it called MAXGEN?</summary><p>It stands for the Maxwell Genealogy Standard, after its author Garlon Maxwell. The name is a mark for the canonical version: use it for anything that validates against the public schemas, but not for a fork that diverges.</p></details>

<details><summary>What is OpenGenealogyAI, and what is The Probable Pedigree?</summary><p>OpenGenealogyAI is the organisation that stewards MAXGEN. The Probable Pedigree is a genealogy platform built on the standard that shows what it makes possible. The standard is designed to outlive any product, including that one: it has no special standing in the change process.</p></details>

<details><summary>Why eleven schemas instead of one?</summary><p>Because documents, people, family links, work, sources, places, groups, occasions, names, machine readings and DNA are different things with different privacy needs. Separating them lets a document be re-read without touching conclusions, lets each family link be stored once instead of on both people, lets one place, group or occasion be described once and pointed at by many claims, and lets DNA be locked down harder than everything else.</p></details>

<details><summary>Who made the podcasts?</summary><p>They were produced with an AI narration tool from scripts written by the standard's author, and they describe MAXGEN v1.11, when there were seven schemas. Much has changed since then: the schema pages and the <a href="/changelog/">changelog</a> on this site are authoritative.</p></details>

<details><summary>How does this relate to FamilySearch, Ancestry, WikiTree and the other sites?</summary><p>They hold the records and the communities; MAXGEN is a format for describing evidence that any of them, and any tool built around them, could read and write. It is not a competing service and it does not replace anyone's database. A MaxPerson can record, with evidence and a confidence, that it is the same person as a profile on FamilySearch, WikiTree or Find a Grave, so research done in different places can be reconciled instead of duplicated. The aim is a shared language, and the more sites speak it the more useful everyone's work becomes.</p></details>

<details><summary>Is MAXGEN trying to replace GEDCOM?</summary><p>No. GEDCOM moves finished trees between programs and has done so for forty years. MAXGEN carries the evidence and reasoning behind a tree, which GEDCOM was never designed to hold. MAXGEN 2.0 gives every GEDCOM 7 construct a home, and a tool can happily support both.</p></details>

<details><summary>How do I follow changes?</summary><p>Every release is recorded in the <a href="/changelog/">changelog</a> with the reason it happened. Proposals are discussed in public on GitHub. A mailing list is planned.</p></details>
